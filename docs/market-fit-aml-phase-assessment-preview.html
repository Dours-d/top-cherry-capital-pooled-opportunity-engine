Top Cherry — Market-fit and AML phase assessment

Date: 15 October 2026
Decision requested: Temporise feature development and validate the business model before building further pooled-capital or live-market functionality.
Assessment status: Preliminary product and regulatory-risk assessment; not legal advice and not a substitute for advice from qualified UK/EU financial-services and AML counsel.

Decision

Pause R1.4 onward and do not activate money collection, pooled-fund custody, execution, investor onboarding, automated personal recommendations, or marketplace-wide live collection during this phase.

Run a bounded market-fit and operating-model assessment first. The product should be tested as a non-custodial economic-research and opportunity-analysis tool. It may explain transmission theses, show sourced observations, link candidates, and simulate constrained allocations with explicit assumptions. It should not accept or control user money, arrange purchases on users' behalf, promise returns, or present a personalized instruction to buy a particular regulated financial instrument.

This is not because AML is already triggered by the current prototype. It is because the phrase coalesce funds to invest in quantity creates a materially different future business. That model could introduce AML, financial-services authorization, client-money, investor-protection, financial-promotion, tax, safeguarding, reporting and governance obligations. The exact perimeter depends on what is pooled, who controls it, where users and assets are located, and whether the platform only informs or also arranges, executes or holds value.

What exists now

The current application is a listing-and-allocation prototype with sample market signals, saved opportunities, user preferences, an eBay account-deletion callback, monitoring, and a quantity planner. The new transmission contracts and thesis evaluator are shared analytical code; they do not collect money or establish a regulated investment service.

The current repository records that no money collection is implemented, that the eBay account-deletion subscription is not an inventory feed, and that the sample three-layer signals are not live evidence. Those boundaries should remain visible in the product and in all user research materials.

The current design is therefore best positioned as:

Decision-support research for economic transmission and secondhand-market opportunities, with simulated capital allocation.

It is not yet safe to position it as a pooled investment platform, fund, broker, marketplace operator, investment adviser, or collective purchasing vehicle.

Why AML is a market-fit gate rather than a feature checkbox

AML controls follow the operating model. A regulated UK firm normally needs a business risk assessment, systems and controls, customer due diligence, an MLRO or nominated officer, monitoring, suspicious-activity reporting and records. The UK government describes customer due diligence as identifying customers and, where relevant, beneficial owners, understanding the purpose and expected activity, and keeping appropriate records. It also describes enhanced diligence for higher-risk circumstances and five-year record retention in the relevant cases. 1 The FCA says that MLR-covered firms include investment managers, stockbrokers, payment institutions, financial advisers, investment firms and other listed financial activities, and that the controls must be proportionate to the size and nature of the services. 2

Those requirements cannot be designed responsibly until the business answers basic product questions:

  • Is the user paying for research, paying a transaction fee, or contributing investment capital?
  • Is the target asset a physical secondhand good, a financial instrument, a share in a pooled vehicle, a loan, a cryptoasset, or a mixture?
  • Does Top Cherry ever receive, hold, route, convert or return funds?
  • Does Top Cherry select a transaction for an identified person, introduce counterparties, negotiate, or execute?
  • Who owns inventory and who bears loss, fraud, delivery, valuation and exit risk?
  • Is the product available only in one jurisdiction or cross-border?
  • Does a user receive a general research output or a recommendation based on personal circumstances?

AML work before those answers would risk building the wrong controls around the wrong business. The first phase should therefore test the narrowest valuable model and map the expansion triggers.

Regulatory boundary signals to preserve

UK FCA perimeter guidance says that carrying on a regulated activity by way of business generally requires authorization or an exemption. It specifically describes arrangements made with a view to another person buying, selling, subscribing for or underwriting investments as an arranging activity. 3

FCA guidance describes investment advice as a personal recommendation to an investor or potential investor that is presented as suitable for that person or based on personal circumstances, concerning a transaction in a financial instrument. It also says that merely providing information normally does not itself amount to investment advice, while selective information or targeted communications can change the analysis. 4

This creates an important product distinction:

  • A public, source-linked thesis about how an economic relationship may affect a market is closer to research or information.
  • A user-specific instruction such as “you should buy this particular financial instrument because of your capital, risk tolerance and circumstances” is much closer to personal advice.
  • A link that simply opens a third-party listing is materially different from taking the order, matching counterparties, receiving payment, or arranging the investment.
  • A simulated quantity plan for physical goods is different from pooled ownership or a financial claim, but it can still create consumer, payments, agency, fraud and financial-promotion issues as the workflow becomes transactional.

For the EU, the Commission describes the European Crowdfunding Service Provider Regulation as covering investment-based and lending-based crowdfunding related to business financing, with authorization, governance, risk-management, disclosure and investor-protection requirements. 5 A model that pools capital for business financing or sells participations cannot be treated as an ordinary marketplace feature merely because the first interface looks like a search engine.

Market-fit questions to answer before more build

1. Who has the urgent problem?

Interview three distinct groups separately: professional or semi-professional resellers, small pooled-buying groups, and research-oriented investors. Do not combine their answers. Their willingness to pay, risk tolerance, operational burden and regulatory exposure differ.

The key test is not whether users like the economic-transmission idea. It is whether a clearly defined user will repeatedly pay for one narrow outcome, such as discovering a market mismatch earlier, comparing cross-market costs, or documenting why a candidate should be rejected.

2. What is the smallest valuable product?

Test a paid or invite-only research workspace with:

  • source-linked observations;
  • transmission theses and competing explanations;
  • candidate links without purchase execution;
  • transparent cost and quantity simulations;
  • saved decisions and later outcome recording;
  • no pooled wallet, no fund transfer, no custody and no automated order.

The first paid workflow should be narrow enough that its value can be measured without pretending to solve portfolio management or live prediction.

3. What evidence would disprove fit?

Stop or reframe the product if users mainly want a conventional price-search tool, if they will not provide outcome feedback, if the strongest demand is for regulated investment recommendations, or if the only credible revenue model requires taking custody of money. A compliance-heavy model with weak willingness to pay is not validated market fit.

Phase AML-0: the next four work packages

AML-0.1 — Freeze the operating boundary

Write a one-page product boundary stating that Top Cherry does not accept, hold, transfer or invest money; does not execute orders; does not guarantee returns; and does not make personal recommendations on financial instruments. Add a visible in-app version of the boundary and a versioned change log.

Do not add KYC screens simply to signal seriousness. KYC is not a substitute for deciding whether the business is an AML-regulated obliged entity or whether a regulated partner will perform the relevant activity.

AML-0.2 — Run market-fit discovery

Conduct structured interviews and a concierge pilot with separate cohorts. Record the user problem, current workaround, decision frequency, willingness to pay, required data, desired action, and the point at which the user expects Top Cherry to take responsibility.

Use anonymized research records. Do not ask participants to send money, financial documents or sensitive personal data during discovery.

AML-0.3 — Map the funds and asset flows

For each operating-model option, draw the flow from user onboarding to payment, ownership, purchase, custody, resale, proceeds and withdrawal. Identify every actor, jurisdiction, account, ledger and failure state. Mark which party performs KYC, sanctions screening, transaction monitoring, safeguarding, execution and reporting.

A flow diagram should be a go/no-go artifact. If a box says “Top Cherry holds funds” or “Top Cherry allocates investments for the user,” the model leaves the research-only phase and needs specialist perimeter advice before implementation.

AML-0.4 — Define the expansion triggers

Escalate to specialist counsel and a compliance design review before any of the following: pooled contributions; a return or profit claim; fractional or shared ownership; custody or wallet balances; direct debit or card collection for investments; automated purchase or sale; matching contributors to an investment opportunity; individualized “buy/hold/sell” outputs; financial-instrument recommendations; cross-border investor onboarding; or access by high-risk jurisdictions.

Go / no-go criteria

Proceed with research-only market-fit testing if there is a specific user segment, repeated pain, a measurable paid outcome, no custody or execution, and communications can remain evidence-linked and non-personalized.

Pause and obtain specialist advice if the value proposition depends on pooled returns, shared ownership, accepting funds, arranging investments, or personalized recommendations. The user need may be real, but that is a different company and control environment.

Do not launch if the team cannot state who owns the asset, who controls the money, who bears loss, how suspicious activity is escalated, or which entity is authorized for the relevant activity.

Recommended next implementation state

Mark R1.4–R5 as paused pending AML-0 and market-fit findings. Preserve the completed R1.1–R1.3 transmission contracts as internal analytical foundations, but do not expose them as a live investment recommendation engine.

The next concrete build should be a research-only assessment workspace: interview hypothesis tracking, evidence/provenance notes, operating-model comparison, and a funds-flow map. It should not add payment, KYC, custody, execution or investor onboarding.
